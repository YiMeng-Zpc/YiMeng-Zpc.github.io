# Urumqi, Xinjiang: A Museum and the Craft of the Bazaar

Urumqi is the capital of the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region, and the world's most landlocked major city (about 2,500 km from any ocean). The name comes from Mongolian for "beautiful pasture."

## A Museum

At Erdaoqiao there is the Urumqi Museum.

Gold moulding runs in loops around the portico, and the wall carries the museum's name in red. The windows are filled with coloured glass — red, green, yellow, like wrapping paper. Visitors stop to take pictures at the door.

Inside is a long corridor laid with red carpet. The columns are painted pink, arches follow one after another, and above them rises a very high circular dome. A lamp hangs in the middle, its tassels moving slightly in the draught.

## Look Up

The one thing worth looking up for.

At the centre of the dome is a round window with coloured glass radiating out from the middle, like a flower opening overhead. Around it runs a ring of smaller arched windows, cutting the light into many pieces and letting it fall on the walls below, which keeps the whole space very quiet.

There were not many people in the hall, but everyone was looking the same way.

## The Bazaar

Coming out of the museum, you are in the bazaar.

Colour is the first thing to hit you. Hangings, carpets, dried fruit, sweets, piled up solid, with parasols turned up overhead in red, yellow and blue, pressing the aisle down low.

Further in, the workshops come one after another.

## Copper

One shop is given over to copper.

The shelves hold kettles and pots of every size — round-bellied, narrow-necked, with a bright ring along the lip. A row of smaller vessels sits on a wooden rack against the wall. A craftsman sits inside with his tools spread in front of him, not looking up much.

## Wood

Another is a woodshop.

The beams are old beams and the walls are old boards. Over the door hangs a wooden plaque carved with three characters — "Zhangde Tai" — with most of the paint flaked away. Inside, a row of counters along the wall carries wooden jars of all sizes, their mouths sealed with cloth. Behind the counter someone works with their head down, slowly.

## Clay

There is also a room of clay figures.

A hall full of small people: one driving a cart, one leading a camel, one holding a child. Each pose is different, and the faces have been reduced to a few large shapes. Further in there is a row of copper pieces, the largest half a person high, with the pattern on the belly worked in lines.

## Cloth

The best stretch, actually, is the cloth.

Bolts of carpeting and fabric run from the counter all the way up the wall in red, green and yellow stripes, packed tightly, as if someone had cut a rainbow into strips. Someone sorts through them in the middle; a flick of the hand opens a whole length, and the row of colours moves with it.

Urumqi is the core hub for Xinjiang travel — nearly every Xinjiang trip starts or ends here. Sixty kilometers further south is the Nanshan pasture, and beyond that a tower marking the geographic centre of the Asian Continent. The city itself has its own beauty — a modern city at the foot of the Tien Shan, contrasting with distant snow mountains and the Gobi.
