# Sayram Lake, Xinjiang: A High-Mountain Blue Lake in the Western Tien Shan

Sayram Lake (Sairam Lake) is the largest cold-water alpine lake in the Tien Shan range, at 2,071 meters elevation. Mongolian herders call it "Sairam Nuur," meaning "the lake on the mountain ridge."

## The Lake

The lake runs about 30 km east-west and 25 km north-south. It feeds on snowmelt from the surrounding peaks, with water temperatures staying below 10°C year-round. Cold-water fish are the local specialty, the most famous being the great whitefish.

Those were clear days, almost no cloud at all. The sky was a heavy blue, the sun hung high, and the light came hard enough to starburst when you squinted. The water changed with it, running from pale turquoise near the shore out to deep blue at the far side. The surface was never still: the wind pushed the swell into line after line of white, and each one broke on the shore.

## The Shore

The lake has no sand beach. It has a shingle beach.

The stones have been worked smooth by the water, and they cover the whole strand in every size — grey, brown, bleached white. They shift underfoot and make a sound when you walk. A wave comes up and sheets over them, then draws back, sorting out whatever was lying in the cracks. Squatting on the stones watching that can take a whole afternoon.

## The Far Shore

Across the water is a ridge, and the ridge is capped with snow.

Above the snow line everything is white; below it the rock is pressed into deep blue-grey by the cloud shadows, and where two ridges overlap you cannot tell which is farther. When the sun cuts in from behind the crest, a very thin white line lights up along the edge of the cliff.

## Higher Up

Leaving the shore, I walked up into the hills.

The slope in front of me was rock bleached pale by the sun, with sparse grass pushing out of the cracks, a dark green. Below was a deep valley, and a road at the bottom of it bent into a very blunt U. Across the road lay a whole range under snow, sliced into layers by the cloud.

The wind was far stronger up here than at the lake. Hair blew constantly to one side, and sometimes the shutter would not fire.

## At Sayram Lake

The ring road around the lake is about 90 km; you can self-drive or hire a car, with viewing platforms every few kilometers. From Urumqi it is about a 6-hour drive; from Yining about 2.5 hours. Most people start from Yining, as part of a deeper Ili Valley trip.

You can stay in Mongolian-run yurts by the lake, and it is worth a night: the lake by day and the lake after dark are two different things. Once the sky goes, the surface takes in every star there is.
