# Yining, Xinjiang: Pigeons, a Fogged Forest, and After the Lights Come On

Yining is the central city of the Ili River valley. Of Xinjiang's major cities, this one least feels like "Xinjiang" — the old city is in no hurry to build tall, the pace is slow, the lanes are narrow, and the plane trees break the sun into pieces on the ground.

## The Pigeons

There is a square downtown, and its main residents are pigeons.

Several hundred of them, half walking on the pavement and half wheeling overhead. Throw something up and they come apart into a sheet of them, wings flashing in the sun; it takes several minutes for them to settle again. The old people at the edge of the square are in no hurry — they squat there with a fistful of grain the whole time.

A woman in red walks through the middle of the flock without turning her head. The buildings behind are new and white, their glass throwing the light back.

## A Stand of Trees Under Fog

Out toward the hills there is a row of spruce by the road.

Planted trees, evenly spaced, standing in a line like a wall. Behind them the hill is completely wrapped in fog — only a vague white shape, no way to judge how high it goes. The air is damp and smells of soil and pine needles.

There is a wire fence around the stand. The trees inside it are doing well, and the grass outside is doing well too, but a line runs between them.

## After the Lights Come On

Once it is dark, the lanes on this side of the old city light up.

First the lanterns: colored ones hung in strings down from the eaves, red and green, washing the whole lane pink. Stalls are set out along the ground, selling food and handicrafts, and people push through the middle.

In one place a large parasol is open at the roadside, with a ring of lights under it, warm yellow, turning the table beside it into a small lit patch. There are also little orange kiosks, lit on all four sides, people sitting inside.

## Indoors

Further in, the ceiling changes.

One place is an open hall with big woven lamps hung from the beams — spherical ones, shade-shaped ones, cane and cloth mixed together, hanging in rows, lit from below with warm light. The stalls are divided by red cloth curtains, bay by bay, like walking into a labyrinth made of cloth.

What is on the stalls is a mixed lot: small wooden things, leather, woven hangings, and many objects whose purpose is not obvious. Most of the sellers do not talk; they just watch you go past.

## In Yining

Yining's pace is much slower than Urumqi's. There is also Kazanchi Folk Village to walk — once a neighborhood of cast-iron cookware makers, now a district of traditional Uyghur homes with blue-purple walls and horse carts in the lanes. Further south, on Six Star Street, an Orthodox church and a mosque stand within a few hundred meters of each other; both are worth a walk.

Get up early for the bazaar, where you can buy fresh Ili apples, raisins and naan.

If you want to go deeper, the Ili Valley surrounds have Sayram Lake, Nalati Grassland and the Khorgos Port — but Yining itself is the gateway to all that slow Ili Valley living.
