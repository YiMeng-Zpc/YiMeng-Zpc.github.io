# Wuerhe Ghost City, Xinjiang: Yardang Landforms and Wind

Wuerhe World Ghost City is in Wuerhe District, Karamay, Xinjiang — a textbook yardang landform, wind-eroded over millions of years into castles, flat-topped mesas and mounds.

## Yardang Landforms

"Yardang" is a Uyghur word meaning "steep little hill." Ghost City is the most famous one. Hundreds of millions of years ago this was a giant freshwater lake; the sedimentary layers were lifted by tectonic movement, then sculpted by the year-round northwest wind from the Junggar Basin. Softer rock wore away; harder rock remained — leaving the castles, mesas and mounds you see today.

The scenic area covers about 10 square kilometers, with a sightseeing tram to help you get around.

## It Was Overcast When I Arrived

The cloud was sitting low when I came in — a seamless grey-blue sky with no gap in it.

That is not what I expected. The way you usually picture Ghost City is dusk, warm light raking in from the side and tracing every mound with a gold edge. There was none of that light. The sun came through the cloud as one flat sheet, shadows stayed shallow, and the earth tones went cold — a yellow-brown with grey in it, the shadows in the gullies a pale blue.

Overcast has its own advantage here. The best thing about wind-eroded rock is the hollows and the holes: the scoops cut out at the foot of a mesa, the horizontal bedding lines, the channels scored by running water. Under direct sun those details get flattened. In this diffuse light every one of them stands out.

## The Shapes

The mounds in the park come in a few forms.

Most common is the flat-topped mesa — level on top, steep on all sides, with visible bedding along the edge like a stack of pancakes. Some of them have lost a corner, and the broken face is redder than the original.

There are also slender, round-topped columns standing alone on the flats, like buns just pushed up out of the ground, with a hollow ring around the base. That is where the wind goes in and out.

Snow appears now and then — not on the ground, but on a far, higher ridge, white against the distance. Desert and snow mountain inside a single frame is the most un-Gobi thing about this place.

## The Wind

The "Ghost" in the name comes from the wind. The Junggar Basin wind pours in from the northwest, channeling between the rocky hills, making all kinds of sounds — sometimes weeping, sometimes horns, mixed with the hiss of sand. Local herders call it "ghosts crying and wolves howling."

The wind was not strong that day, but passing through the mounds you could still hear air squeezing through the narrow gaps, low, like someone blowing a badly tuned horn a long way off.

## In Wuerhe

From Karamay city it is about an hour's drive. Most visitors and photographers stay in Karamay or Wuerhe town, enter the park at sunset, and return at dawn the next day for sunrise. Outside the park there are paragliding and 4x4 tours, but the wind-eroded landscape alone is worth the ticket.
