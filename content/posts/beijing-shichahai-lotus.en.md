# Lotus at Shichahai: Dark Green Water

There is almost no "Shichahai" in this set. It is all things seen while looking down at the water.

## The water is dark

The surface is nearly black, and the lotus leaves float on it in a heavy green.

In several frames there is nothing but a leaf and a little water: veins spreading out from the centre, the rim slightly curled, a few white specks on the dark surface. The light is not bright, but the colours hold — deep green, dark teal, a touch of pink, all squeezed into one narrow range.

The centre of a leaf is hollow. Once a droplet rolls in it cannot get out, and the little pool that gathers there presses a bright patch into the whole leaf. You can almost feel the weight of it through the photograph.

## Pink flowers

The flowers come up through the gaps between leaves.

Some have only two or three petals open, the colour strongest at the tips and fading to near-white lower down. Some are fully open, with the yellow centre exposed and the fine stamens visible. After a while you notice that changing the angle of the same flower can turn the background from deep green to total black — the water takes all the light, and only the flower is bright.

In one frame a slender damselfly rests on a stem, wings almost transparent, its body barely wider than the stalk. There is also an orange flower against a very dark ground, not a lotus at all, but lit like a small lamp.

After the flower goes, what remains is the seed pod, brown, its holes countable one by one, the stem still straight.

## One bird

A black waterbird stands on a floating leaf. Thin legs, neck pumping in and out.

The leaf sinks under its weight, the edge slipping into the water, and the smaller leaves nearby rock with it. It did not stay long; when it left, a ring of ripples spread slowly outward.

In another frame the foreground blurs into a sheet of white and a pink lotus shows through from behind it, as if seen through something.

## Looking up, only at the end

Only in the very last frame does the camera pull back: the whole stretch of water, boats along the bank, trees and a long row of buildings opposite, a grey-white sky with no blue in it.

So that is Shichahai. The first nineteen frames were one corner of it; the last one gives it back.

Looking through them afterwards, what stayed with me was the droplets, the leaf veins and the bird pressing a leaf under water — not the lake.
