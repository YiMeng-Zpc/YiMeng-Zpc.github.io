# Sunset at the Shougang Bridge: A White Arch in the Haze

From this end of the road, looking west, the bridge is easy to pick out: two white arch feet standing on either side of the traffic, with nothing strung between them.

## One road, two layers of hills

Behind the bridge there are hills.

Not one solid wall but layers. The nearest ridge is darkest and you can read its ups and downs; the next is paler; the one behind that almost dissolves into the sky. The haze pulled the distances apart, so they look much further away than they are.

A few frames are vertical, the arch centred with the hills pressed inside it and a string of headlights along the bottom. Turn horizontal and the bridge becomes something small, while the hills and the sky take over the whole picture.

## A sunset without edges

The sun that day was not a clean disc. It was a blurred bright spot in the haze.

That softened everything: orange and pink smeared outward, clouds drawn into fine threads across the upper half, and the buildings, poles and roadside trees reduced to silhouettes with no hard edges. It was like shooting through a thin veil — every colour half a stop quieter.

It is not the best weather for a sunset. There is none of that crisp gold rim. But the haze is exactly why the ridges separate so cleanly, and why the white of the bridge never turns glarey.

## Traffic under the arch

The bridge does not move. The traffic does.

Once the tail lights came on, red lines dragged away towards the middle of the frame. In the longer exposures the lights join into unbroken threads, and the arch becomes the quietest thing in the picture.

There is also a high-voltage pylon nearby, shot vertically: nothing but fine black lattice work in the sunset, tier after tier narrowing upward, its tip almost touching the pink sky. It has nothing to do with the bridge, but they share the same intersection, so they ended up in the same frame.

## The last of the light

As the sky dropped further, orange-pink gave way to grey-violet and the last ridge was nearly gone. The white of the bridge only grew clearer — it gives off no light of its own, it just holds what is left on its surface.

On the way back the streetlights were all on. The arch was still standing there, as if waiting for the next day.
