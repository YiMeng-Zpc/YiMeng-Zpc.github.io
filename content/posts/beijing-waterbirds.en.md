# Birds by the Water: Yuyuantan and Shichahai

Two trips to the water, and the camera hardly left the surface. One to Yuyuantan at the end of March, one to Shichahai in May.

## On the bank

The first pictures were not of the water at all.

On the sandy ground in front of the trees, a few pigeons walked, grey and pale, necks pumping, shadows short. The trunk behind them blurred into a coarse grain, which left the birds as the sharpest things in the frame.

A couple of dark ducks crouched on the shallows too, almost the colour of wet mud, easy to miss.

## Mandarin ducks

The male is unmistakable: a white eyebrow, green on the head, and orange sail-shaped feathers standing up on either side, like two pieces someone slotted in.

As he swims towards you, the bow wave splits into a very tidy V. The reflections break up, gold and blue-white strips rocking along with it.

In a few frames he has come ashore and is standing on a stone at the water's edge: wings folded, feet showing, head turned exactly towards the camera. In that moment he looks less like a swimming bird than like something waiting. Other frames are quieter — brown-grey birds floating without moving, a ring of ripples spreading slowly behind them.

## White swans

Then a set of large white birds.

The water is blue and the birds are white, the boundary almost too clean to be real. The neck bends into a question mark and the reflection bends with it; some lower their heads to the surface, some push them right under, leaving only a white back and an expanding ring.

I took many frames of the same pose. The only differences are the angle of the neck and how far the wind has crumpled the reflection.

## Frames with no bird

A few have no bird in them at all.

One round floating leaf, its rim curled, holds a load of droplets with the veins lit from the centre — the single subject in the frame. In others the water is pushed into silver threads by the wind, and after the bird swims off only the ripples keep widening.

These turned out not to be rejects. Waiting is what takes the most time when you photograph birds, and during the waiting the water changes on its own.

## Waiting

Sorting them out afterwards, most of the set is bursts: the bird has not changed posture, the light has not changed, only the pattern on the water is different in every frame.

They do not conveniently swim into position when you press the shutter. So this set is less about "photographing birds" than about having stayed by the water a long while.
